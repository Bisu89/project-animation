# Ep 1 Thermopylae: source notes

Draft script: `script.md`. Evidence category for each claim, so the narration never states a tradition as fact.

## Well attested (Herodotus Book 7, some Diodorus/Plutarch)
- 480 BC invasion, bridge of boats over the Hellespont. (Hdt 7.33-37)
- Narrow pass, repaired Phocian wall, Leonidas with 300 Spartans who had living sons, about 7,000 Greeks in all. (Hdt 7.175-177, 7.202-205)
- Xerxes waited four days, scout saw Spartans exercising and combing hair, Demaratus explained. (Hdt 7.208-209)
- Medes, then Immortals, failed on days one and two; Xerxes leapt from his throne. (Hdt 7.210-212)
- Ephialtes of Trachis showed the Anopaia path for reward; Hydarnes and the Immortals took it at night; 1,000 Phocians guarded it and withdrew to a hilltop. (Hdt 7.213-218)
- Leonidas sent the allies away; Thespians and Thebans stayed; Thebans surrendered. (Hdt 7.219-222, 7.222, 7.233)
- Last stand beyond the wall, spears broke, fight over Leonidas's body (four times), last stand on a hill, hands and teeth, arrows. (Hdt 7.223-225)
- Xerxes mutilated the body, which Herodotus calls out of character. (Hdt 7.238)
- Survivors Eurytus (returned and died), Aristodemus (shamed), Pantites (messenger, shamed). (Hdt 7.229-232)
- Simonides epitaph. (Hdt 7.228)
- Ephialtes fled, price on head, later killed over an unrelated quarrel. (Hdt 7.213, 7.214)

## Disputed or later, so wording is hedged ("some ancient writers", "Herodotus says")
- Feigned retreat tactic: Diodorus 11.7, not in Herodotus.
- Dienekes "fight in the shade": Herodotus 7.226 attributes it to him; the remark itself is probably a later anecdote.
- Oracle that Sparta or its king must fall: Herodotus 7.220; likely hindsight.
- Herodotus gives Persian army as 2.6 million+ (7.184-186). Modern estimates: roughly 100,000-300,000 (varies; some lower). Script says "between one hundred thousand and a few hundred thousand".
- "Fewer than one in twenty": 300 / 7,000, using Herodotus's figure (about 4%).
- Greek force at the end "about fourteen hundred" = 300 Spartans + 700 Thespians + 400 Thebans; excludes helots and other attendants, the seer Megistias, and any others who may have stayed. Stated as "about".
- Persian shields wicker, short spears: Herodotus 7.61, 7.211.

## Check before voicing
1. "Salamis a few weeks later, Plataea a year after": Salamis was Sept 480, Plataea Aug 479. OK.
2. Immortals "ten thousand strong": Herodotus 7.83.
3. Phocian reaction ("saved the enemy a fight"): Herodotus says Persians ignored them and went on. Wording is our reading, not his.
4. Pronunciations for the voice: Thermopylae (ther-MOP-ih-lee), Ephialtes (eff-ee-AL-teez), Anopaia (an-oh-PIE-ah), Kallidromon, Dienekes (dye-EN-eh-keez), Demaratus, Hydarnes, Thespiae (THES-pee-ee), Trachis.

## Added in the long analytic version (3,600 words, about 24 min at 150 wpm)
Clearly our own analysis, not claims from Herodotus; script words them as "may", "look at it as" or "engineers have a name for this":
- Frontage arithmetic (1 m per man, 15-20 m gap) is an illustration, flagged "for the sake of the picture". Herodotus only says the narrowest points fit one wagon.
- Hoplite kit about 25 kg: commonly cited range 20-30 kg.
- "Single point of failure" framing and the rearguard-to-cover-retreat reading of Leonidas's choice are modern interpretations.
- Two-front plan (Thermopylae + Artemisium protect each other): Hdt 7.175, 8.1-21. Fleet withdrew after hearing the pass fell: Hdt 8.21.
- Spartan festival (Carneia) explaining the small force: Hdt 7.206. Phocians volunteered for the path: Hdt 7.217. Locals told the Greeks about the path: Hdt 7.175.
- Xerxes buried most of his dead, left 1,000 visible: Hdt 8.24-25. Herodotus himself presents it as a transparent trick. The script's "a man who hides his casualties knows what they look like" is our comment.
- Persian arrowheads found on the Kolonos hill (Marinatos, 1939). Verify wording before voicing.
- Aristodemus at Plataea: Hdt 9.71. Athenades and the reward: Hdt 7.213.
- Artemisium "bruising draw": Hdt 8.15-18; verify wording.
- Immortals as the column on the Anopaia: Herodotus says "Hydarnes and the Persians under him"; the 10,000 figure is the usual Immortals strength (Hdt 7.83). Soften if challenged.
- Athos canal and 492 BC storm: Hdt 6.44, 7.22-24. Years of stockpiled food depots: Hdt 7.25. Rivers drunk dry: Hdt 7.21, 7.108-109.
