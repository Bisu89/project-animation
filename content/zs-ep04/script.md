# 1. The Room

On the fourth day I did not leave the room. That was also a decision.

I want to be honest about it. I told myself it was strategy. The street had been busy since the night before, and rule one says noise draws them, and the safest thing was to be nothing at all. All of that was true. It was also true that I was afraid, and that fear had found a way to sound reasonable.

The utility building is four metres by five. Concrete floor, a steel door, a hatch at the back, a ceiling I could touch if I stood on a crate. I knew every crack in it by the afternoon.

I had a little less than three-quarters of a litre of water, no food, and a hand that had almost stopped hurting. My stomach had stopped complaining around midday. That was not a good sign.

# 2. A Sip at a Time

I made a rule that was not on my phone. One sip, every hour. Count to ten, hold it in your mouth, swallow slowly.

It sounds like discipline. It is closer to a trick. The hour passes while you wait for the sip, and the sip is something to wait for.

I sat with my back against the wall and watched the sliver of light under the door move across the floor. When it reached the third crack I allowed myself the next mouthful. When it reached the end of the crate, I allowed myself one more.

Hunger is not a pain. It is a sound. A low hum that never stops and never changes, and after a while you cannot tell whether it is your body or the room.

# 3. The Street Outside

Around noon I heard them. First a few. Then more, far off, then closer, a loose slow shuffle that moved along the wall of the building and did not stop.

They did not know I was here. They were not coming for me. They were passing, the way a river passes a stone, and the stone does not choose to be a stone.

I stopped breathing through my mouth. I put my hand flat against the cold concrete and counted. Fifteen, twenty. The shuffle continued, and a long pale shadow slid across the gap beneath the door, and then another, and then another.

I counted sixty shadows.

When the last one passed I realised I had bitten the inside of my cheek, and the taste of blood was a relief, because it was something that was mine.

# 4. The Rules

I took out the phone and read them again.

Noise draws them. Be quiet. Never go into a place without a second way out. Do not stay anywhere you cannot leave in under a minute.

I read the third rule, the newest one, and understood what I had done. I was in a building I could leave in ten seconds. I had broken none of them. And I had not moved in a day.

That frightened me more than the shadows. The rules had kept me alive. They could keep me alive until I starved. A list of things I must not do tells you how to hide. It does not tell you how to live.

I did not write a fourth rule. I only sat there with the phone at forty percent, and the screen going dark, and a word I could not finish.

# 5. The Night

It came back at night. One of them, alone.

I heard it long before it reached the door. A slow, uneven step, and a drag. It stopped outside. For a long time nothing happened.

Then a hand on the steel. Not pushing. Testing. Fingers along the edge of the frame, the way you feel for a light switch in the dark.

I did not move. I did not breathe. I did not even look at the door. I looked at the floor, at a single crack in the concrete, and I held that crack in my mind like a rope.

The hand slid down the door. It found the handle. It pulled once, uselessly. It pushed once, and the door held.

I thought: if I make one sound, it will not matter how thick the steel is. I thought: if I make no sound, it will not matter at all.

It stayed for a long time. Then, slowly, it turned away.

# 6. The Plan

I could not sleep after that. So I used the light.

Not the flashlight. The phone, turned to its lowest brightness, cupped in my hand against my chest. I spent two percent of the battery, and I wrote a plan.

A route. A time limit: two hours out, the same back. A place to look: the shops two streets east, where I had seen a pharmacy sign. A retreat: if I heard noise, I would go left, along the wall, into the lane behind the bakery.

At the bottom I wrote one line that was not a rule.

Tomorrow I leave.

I read it until the screen went dark. And then I lay down on the cold floor and for the first time in four days, I slept without dreaming.
