# Short 1 - How One Man Ended the Battle of Thermopylae

The Persian army was so big that Herodotus said it drank rivers dry.

The Greeks did not try to beat it. They found a road the width of one cart.

Here, a hundred thousand men could only fight about twenty at a time.

For two days, the Persians sent in their best soldiers, and lost.

Then a local man named Ephialtes walked into the Persian camp. He knew a mountain path nobody had mapped.

A thousand Greeks were guarding it. It was not enough.

Thermopylae was never lost in a fair fight. It was lost when somebody showed the enemy a way around.

Count how many can reach each other. And count the paths nobody is watching.
