# Biblical Figures — 15 Episode Outline

The outlines below condense the owner's supplied notes. “Recorded” means present in the cited religious or historical source; it does not by itself mean independently verified. Later traditions and scholarly disputes must be attributed in the script.

| # | Subject / working hook | Core material and editorial focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Judas Iscariot — Thirty pieces of silver, one kiss, and two endings** | Betrayal narratives; Matthew and Acts give different accounts of his death; distinguish the later Gnostic Gospel of Judas from the canonical texts. Marked complete in the supplied notes. |
| 2 | **Pontius Pilate — The Roman prefect caught between order and revolt** | Caesarea inscription; prefecture and reported incidents; Jesus' trial; recall after the Samaritan episode; later Pilate legends. Marked complete. |
| 3 | **Mary Magdalene — The witness history turned into a sinner** | Her role in the four Gospels and at the empty tomb; Gregory I's 591 conflation and its later correction; Gospel of Mary; French hermit traditions; reject the unsupported “wife of Jesus” claim as fact. Marked complete. |
| 4 | **Simon Peter — From denying Jesus to leading a movement** | Gospel episodes, restoration, Pentecost, Cornelius, and conflict with Paul; distinguish New Testament accounts from Roman martyrdom traditions and later relic claims. Marked complete. |
| 5 | **Paul of Tarsus — The persecutor who carried a new movement across the empire** | Stephen, Damascus, Ananias, journeys, Athens, Ephesus, Malta, Rome; identify the seven widely accepted letters and the disputed authorship of other letters. Marked complete. |
| 6 | **John the Baptist — The prophet executed under Herod** | Birth and ministry traditions; baptism of Jesus; compare Mark's execution story with Josephus' political explanation; later relic claims. Marked complete. |
| 7 | **Caiaphas — The high priest who held office through a decade of Roman rule** | Annas and family influence; trial narratives; Josephus on appointments and removal; the Caiaphas ossuary and its debated identification; present political and theological readings without flattening them. Marked complete. |
| 8 | **Herod Antipas — The ruler who executed a prophet and mocked a prisoner** | John the Baptist's execution; Jesus sent to Antipas in Luke; Tiberias; defeat by Aretas and Josephus' report of popular interpretation; exile after appealing to Caligula. |
| 9 | **Thomas — The doubter remembered in India** | Thomas in John, including his earlier courage; the third-century Acts of Thomas and the Saint Thomas Christian community in Kerala; distinguish community tradition, later text, and what trade archaeology can establish. |
| 10 | **James, Brother of Jesus — From unbelief to leader in Jerusalem** | Competing Christian interpretations of “brothers”; appearance tradition in 1 Corinthians; leadership in Acts and Galatians; Josephus' account of James' death in 62 CE. Explain the limits of each source. |
| 11 | **Barabbas — The man released, then lost to history** | Gospel accounts of the prisoner release; revolt and violence; meaning of “Barabbas”; textual variant “Jesus Barabbas”; debate over whether the release custom is historically attested. Make the absence of evidence part of the story. |
| 12 | **Nicodemus and Joseph of Arimathea — Two insiders at the burial** | Nicodemus in John 3 and 7; Joseph's request for the body and tomb; later Glastonbury/Grail tradition and its medieval setting. |
| 13 | **Mary, Mother of Jesus — A few textual appearances, many centuries of interpretation** | Luke and Matthew infancy narratives, Jesus at twelve, Cana, crucifixion, Acts 1; compare Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant teachings with careful attribution; do not treat doctrine as settled historical fact. |
| 14 | **Herod the Great — The builder and the disputed massacre** | Josephus on his rule, building, family executions, and Roman ties; Matthew's Bethlehem massacre and its absence from other surviving sources; present historical and literary readings; astronomical proposals for the star remain hypotheses. |
| 15 | **Stephen — The first martyr, watched by Paul** | Acts 6–8; Stephen's speech and death; Saul/Paul's presence; close the season by connecting the persecution in Stephen's story with Paul's later mission. Consider moving this episode directly after #5. |
