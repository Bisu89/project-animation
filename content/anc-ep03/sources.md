# Ep 3 Alesia: source notes

Draft script: `script.md` (about 2,950 words, about 19 min). Evidence categories.

## Well attested (Caesar, De Bello Gallico Book 7, written by Caesar himself; Plutarch, Caesar 27; Cassius Dio 40, 43)
- 52 BC revolt under Vercingetorix, scorched-earth plan; Caesar repulsed at Gergovia with heavy losses (about 700 men, 46 centurions). BG 7.4, 7.14-15, 7.36-52.
- Cavalry fight on Caesar's retreat, German horse defeat the Gauls; Vercingetorix withdraws to Alesia. BG 7.65-68.
- Alesia: hill town of the Mandubii with two rivers and a plain in front; Vercingetorix with about 80,000 infantry (Caesar's figure). BG 7.69-71.
- Vercingetorix sends his cavalry away to raise a general levy; food for about 30 days. BG 7.71.
- Caesar chooses blockade rather than assault. BG 7.68.
- Inner line 11 Roman miles, outer line 14, trench 20 ft with vertical sides, two trenches 15 ft wide/deep (inner one flooded), rampart 12 ft with palisade, towers every 80 ft, cippi, lilia, stimuli. Soldiers to gather 30 days' grain and fodder. BG 7.72-74.
- The Mandubii expelled and refused entry by Caesar; Critognatus's speech. BG 7.77-78.
- Relief army: Caesar gives 250,000 foot and 8,000 horse under four leaders (Commius, Viridomarus, Eporedorix, Vercassivellaunus). BG 7.75-76.
- Day 1 cavalry battle (German horse decisive). BG 7.79-80. Day 2 midnight assault with a sally; Antony and Trebonius commanding sectors. BG 7.81-82. Day 3 attack on the northern hill camp by 60,000 picked men; Labienus, then Caesar in his scarlet cloak; cavalry sent round to the rear; Gauls break. BG 7.83-88.
- Surrender and prisoners as booty; Aedui and Arverni prisoners kept. BG 7.89.
- Vercingetorix at Caesar's feet in full armour: Plutarch, Caesar 27 (about 150 years later). Triumph of 46 BC and execution: Cassius Dio 43.19.
- Excavations: Napoleon III's digs (1861-65) and the Franco-German digs (1991-97) found ditches and pits matching the description. Verify wording before voicing.

## Disputed or interpretation (script hedges)
- All numbers are Caesar's own. Relief army of 250,000 is almost certainly inflated (modern estimates around 100,000 or fewer); Roman army size is not given by Caesar (modern estimates 50,000-60,000 including auxiliaries; stated as "perhaps").
- Caesar wrote the account himself, in the third person: script jokes that it is "an ancient press release". Treat his portrait of Roman restraint, the Mandubii and Critognatus with care.
- Whether the siege works were built in "about a month" is uncertain; script says "a matter of weeks".
- The fate of the Mandubii is not recorded by Caesar; script says so.
- Plutarch's surrender scene (riding round Caesar, sitting silent) differs from Caesar's own plain account; script says "we do not know if that is true".
- Dating of the revolt and details of Gergovia differ in modern reconstructions of the campaign.

## Our own analysis (not in the sources)
- "Interior lines" explanation (shorter moves for the side in the middle) and the claim that the Gauls could not coordinate through four leaders versus one Roman commander. The term "interior lines" is a modern military term.
- The comparison with Cannae (divided Roman command) and the series thesis about space ("numbers decide nothing until they are in the same place at the same time").
- The remark that the Romans were "the filling in a sandwich".
