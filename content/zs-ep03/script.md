# 1. No Water

On the morning of day three, my water bottle was empty.

I'd finished it during the night.

My mouth was dry, and I had a headache. When I stood up, I felt dizzy and held the wall until it passed.

My ankle was still swollen. My hand hurt when I closed it.

Yesterday, I'd walked to the evacuation point and back for nothing. Today, I needed to find water close to the room.

I couldn't manage another long trip.

# 2. Checking the Taps

I put the empty bottle in my bag and went outside.

There was a small sink behind the shops.

I turned the tap.

A little air came out, followed by a few brown drops. Then nothing.

I tried another tap on a nearby house. That one was dry too.

I didn't know whether the water had been shut off or something had stopped working.

Either way, I wasn't getting any from those taps.

There was water in the gutter, but it was dirty. I left it alone.

I needed somewhere water might still be stored.

# 3. The Restaurant

A small restaurant behind the shops had its back door open.

I listened outside, then stepped into the kitchen.

There were pans on the stove and plates beside the sink.

I tried the stove. The knob clicked, but no gas came out.

I checked the tap. Nothing.

The cupboards held cleaning products and a few empty food containers. I found no bottled drinks.

At the end of a short corridor was a toilet.

I remembered that the tank behind it held water separately from the bowl. I went to check.

As I lifted the lid, the ceramic scraped against the edge.

I stopped.

After listening for a while, I wrapped some cloth around the lid and lifted it carefully.

The water inside was bright blue.

There was a cleaning tablet at the bottom.

I put the lid back.

I wasn't drinking that.

I checked the remaining cupboard on my way out. Still nothing useful.

I'd spent time searching and had no water to show for it.

# 4. The Clinic

Across the lane was a small clinic.

I'd passed it before. A clinic would have a staff room and bathrooms, and it was small enough to check quickly. This time, I walked around the outside.

The front entrance was glass. A metal side door opened onto the lane.

At the back, three infected stood between parked cars.

I stayed behind a dumpster and watched.

They weren't coming toward me. The corner of the building blocked their view of the side door.

I could leave them alone if I stayed quiet.

I tried the side-door handle.

Unlocked.

Inside was a short corridor leading toward reception.

Before searching, I went to the front entrance and checked it. That door opened too.

I closed it again and locked it from the inside, so nothing could push it open from the street.

Now I knew both exits worked.

I returned to the side door and wedged it open with folded cardboard. Then I started checking the rooms nearby.

# 5. The Water Tank

There was enough daylight to see without the flashlight.

The first room held cleaning supplies. The next was a staff room with an empty water cooler.

Beside it were three empty plastic bottles with their caps.

I took them.

In a drawer, I found a box of sugar sachets. I put those in my bag too.

There was no proper food.

Farther along the corridor, I found a utility room.

Inside stood a large hot-water tank.

I'd seen a similar one drained in a place I used to rent. I hadn't done the job myself, but I remembered there had been a lot of water inside.

This one might still hold some.

I found the drain near the bottom and positioned my bottle beneath it. I turned the valve by hand.

When I tried it, the pipe gave several loud knocks.

I stopped immediately.

I could still picture the three infected behind the building.

I stood listening.

No footsteps in the corridor. No noise from either entrance.

After a while, I tried again, opening it only slightly.

This time, water trickled into the bottle without more knocking.

# 6. Filling the Bottles

The first water looked cloudy. I let that run into a bucket already on the floor.

The water after it looked clearer.

That didn't tell me whether it was safe to drink.

I had no way to check it. I had a lighter, but the restaurant stove didn't work, and a fire would mean smoke and light I didn't want to risk.

I thought about looking somewhere else.

But I'd found nothing at the shops or the restaurant, and my ankle was already hurting more.

I drank some.

Then I filled my bottle and the three I'd found in the staff room.

I kept stopping to listen.

The water ran slowly. By the time I finished, I'd been inside much longer than planned.

I had four full bottles.

I should have packed them and left.

# 7. A Few More Minutes

Instead, I stayed.

I was tired, and I didn't want to leave water behind when I might struggle to find more.

I sat beside the tank and drank again.

Then I rested my ankle.

I told myself I'd leave in a minute.

Nothing had happened since the pipes knocked. That made it easier to keep waiting.

My bag was still open. The bottles were on the floor.

When I finally started packing, I heard a thump from reception.

Then another.

Something was hitting the front door.

# 8. The Side Exit

I looked down the corridor.

Through reception, I could see figures against the glass.

I couldn't tell whether they were the ones from the car park.

I didn't wait to count.

I put three bottles in my bag. That was all I could carry without slowing down. The fourth was still beside the tank.

Another impact shook the front door.

I left the bottle, pulled the bag onto my shoulder, and headed for the side exit.

The cardboard was still holding it open.

As I reached the door, I heard glass break behind me.

I stepped outside, pulled out the cardboard, and closed the door.

Then I moved away from the car park.

The bottles knocked together in my bag.

I stopped behind a parked van and pushed my spare shirt between them.

I listened.

There were noises from the clinic, but no footsteps coming along the lane.

I kept moving.

# 9. Rule Three

At the next corner, I stopped behind a wall.

I'd got out through the second exit I'd checked.

That part had worked.

But I'd wasted time sitting beside the water when I could already have left.

I opened my notes.

Rule three: Once you have what you need, leave.

I hadn't needed to empty the tank.

I hadn't needed to rest inside that building.

I'd needed water, and I'd had it packed within reach.

Staying longer had cost me a bottle and nearly trapped me.

# 10. The Way Back

From behind a van on the route home, I could see the clinic entrance.

The front glass was broken.

Several infected were inside reception, with more standing outside.

I didn't know whether the pipes had attracted them or they'd simply wandered over.

I wasn't going back to check.

There was still water in the tank, but reaching it now meant entering a building occupied by them.

I'd have to find another source before these bottles ran out.

I took the quieter streets back to the room.

My ankle hurt, so I walked slowly and kept the bag close to my back.

# 11. Back Inside

I checked outside the room, went in, and slid the bolt across.

Then I set the bottles on the floor.

Three bottles, about half a liter each.

I'd also had a drink at the clinic.

It was the first time that morning I could sit down without immediately thinking about getting water.

I still didn't know whether drinking it would make me sick.

There was nothing I could do about that now.

I counted the sugar sachets. Around thirty.

I ate one, then put the rest away.

I still needed food. Sugar wasn't going to replace meals.

My ankle was more swollen after the walk. I took off my shoe and rested my foot.

Tomorrow, I would have to keep any trip short.

Before lying down, I packed the bottles so they couldn't knock together. I put the lighter and flashlight where I could reach them.

Today hadn't fixed everything.

But I'd brought water back, and I'd used the second exit instead of getting stuck inside.

For now, that was enough to let me rest.

That night, footsteps passed outside the door.

They stopped for a while.

I stayed quiet.

After a few moments, they moved on.

Later, my stomach cramped.

It could have been the water. It could just as easily have been hunger. I couldn't tell which.

I lay still on the cardboard, listening to the door.

If it was the water, I would know by morning. And if I was sick, I wouldn't be going anywhere tomorrow.
