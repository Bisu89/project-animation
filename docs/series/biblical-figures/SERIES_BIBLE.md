# Biblical Figures — Series Bible

## Series premise

An English-language history and biography series about people connected to the Bible and the early Christian world. Each episode asks what the surviving sources say, what later traditions added, and what remains uncertain. The tone is cinematic, curious, and even-handed across religious traditions.

The 15 episode subjects and the research notes for them come from the owner's document “Biblical Figures — ghi chú 15 tập”. Episodes 1–7 are marked complete in those notes; this repository currently contains the series plan, not their original production projects or media assets.

## Editorial rules

1. Separate claims into three categories: biblical text, external historical evidence, and later tradition/legend. Name the source and date when useful.
2. Do not present a tradition or disputed interpretation as established history. Use clear attribution (“Matthew says…”, “Josephus reports…”, “later tradition holds…”).
3. Present live scholarly disputes fairly, including the evidence and limits on both sides. Do not decide questions the sources cannot settle.
4. Keep the tone respectful toward believers and nonbelievers. Explain denominational doctrines as doctrines held by the relevant communities, not as historical findings.
5. Avoid inventing dialogue, private thoughts, or scenes as fact. Any reconstruction must be clearly signalled as an illustration.
6. Keep episodes distinct: use the assigned hook and subject; cross-reference other episodes when it helps, rather than retelling their central material.
7. Reuse existing art only after checking the image pool, character identity, visual style, and rights/provenance. Record reused assets in the episode spec.

## Episode format

- Audience: international YouTube viewers.
- Narration and on-screen text: English. Production discussion and planning documents may be Vietnamese.
- Target: about 8–13 minutes, set by the approved script and actual narration timing.
- Structure: hook, source-grounded life/context, key turning point, later reception or historical debate, and a closing thought tied to the evidence.
- Visual direction: cinematic AI-generated still images with smooth zoom/pan, English narration, subtitles, and restrained “living picture” effects (light flicker, dust, rain, smoke, subtle environmental motion). Use the same production approach as `100-days-zombie-survival`; choose a historical painting/illustration look appropriate to each scene, not stickman animation.
- Violence: restrained, non-graphic depiction; communicate through distance, gesture, setting, and aftermath.
