# Short 3 - The Wall With Two Sides

At Alesia, Caesar had fifty thousand men. Inside the town were eighty thousand Gauls. Outside came a relief army even bigger.

So he built a wall to keep the Gauls in. Then a second wall, facing the other way, to keep the others out.

Trenches. Sharpened stakes. Pits. Iron hooks buried in the ground.

His army stood in the gap between the two walls.

When the Gauls hit one point, Caesar moved reserves along the short inside route.

The Gauls had four commanders and no single plan.

Caesar had one.

For the Gauls the wall was a prison. For the Romans, a fortress.
